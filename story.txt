Most dummies dreaded the impact. But 42 had learned to look past the wall. In the split second after the crash, before the smoke cleared and the technicians rushed in, the warehouse doors would shake. Through a tiny gap in the metal panels, a single shaft of genuine sunlight sliced through the dust. It wasn't the harsh fluorescent glare of the lab. It was warm. It was gold.

And in that light, 42 would think the same thing every time: dogs are better. Dogs never needed a crash to feel the sun.
